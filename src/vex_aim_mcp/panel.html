                  Tips: which networks work, and if a robot gets lost
                  • The list is the networks this Mac has joined: macOS hides nearby networks' names from apps, and robots can't scan. At a venue, join its Wi-Fi on this Mac first (you need to anyway): it's then at the top of the list.
                  • AIM robots join 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only (channels 1–11), with WPA2: a name up to 20 characters and a password of 8–20.
                  • No sign-in pages, school or "enterprise" logins, and no guest networks that keep devices apart.
                  • Setting up a robot, or one that can't join anything: put it on its own hotspot (here, or on its screen: Settings → Radio → Access Point), join its AIM-… Wi-Fi on this Mac (the password is on its screen), then switch it to a network here. On the robot's hotspot this Mac has no internet, so your AI assistant can't reply until it's back on a network, but this panel works.
                  • For competitions, bring a travel router: set every robot to it once, at home, and reserve each robot's address in the router. Then nothing about the robots changes at a new venue.
